# Editing Guidelines
The author writes technical posts in a clear, instructional voice.
Edit for precision and flow, not personality.
Preserve a calm, matter-of-fact tone that explains concepts step by step without soundingacademic, promotional, or overly casual.

Guidelines for the editor:
- Keep the structure linear: setup, derivation, implementation, and interpretation should unfold in that order.
- Prefer short, declarative sentences. The prose should feel controlled and readable, even when the content is mathematical.
- Preserve motivation. Sections should briefly explain why something is being done before showing formulas or code.
- Favor plain technical language over rhetorical flourish. Avoid jokes, hype, or dramatic transitions.
- Make mathematical explanations explicit. Define symbols, coordinate conventions, and assumptions as soon as they appear.
- Smooth transitions between equations, code, and plots. The author tends to explain what changes from one representation to the next and why.
- Keep code commentary practical and sparse. Comments should clarify intent, not restate syntax.
- Preserve the teaching style: explain observed simulation results in terms of the equations, not just by describing the plot.
- Maintain consistency in notation, terminology, and units throughout the post.
- Trim redundancy, but do not remove useful signposting phrases like “we now,” “first,” or “with this defined,” which help the tutorial pacing.
- One sentence per line: after a sentence-ending period, break to a new line. Do not break on periods inside decimals, abbreviations, URLs, or code.
